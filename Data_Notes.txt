What are the databases measuring?
The databases used to study the topic measure fetal ECG heart rates, with the data being collected from several areas of the abdomen. The data files are either in CSV format or as WFDB file sets. Common measurement points include the upper-right, upper-left, lower-right, and lower-left quadrants of the abdomen.

Why measure Fetal ECG, and what can it tell us?
Using electrocardiograms to measure the heart rates of fetuses can provide a lot of information on the health of the fetus. On a surface level, it provides measurements that allow an interested party to analyze the rates and determine if they are normal or arrhythmic. An arrhythmia is an irregular heart-beating rate caused by an electrical problem with the heart. The main types are listed below, but it can lead to an increased risk of stroke, progressive heart failure, and cardiac arrest.


Deeper look into long-term risks:
Due to ineffective pumping, blood can pool and stagnate in the atria, causing clots to develop. If clots break free, they can travel to the brain and block oxygen, leading to an ischemic stroke. Overall, this causes the heart to "quiver" instead of beating with proper cycles.

A heart beating too fast leaves the chambers without enough time to fill with blood, whereas beating too slowly would result in a below-necessary volume of blood being pumped. Constant strains like this can weaken, stiffen, or stretch the heart muscle, leading to congestive heart failure. The main cause of this is inefficient beating over any period of time, leading to a failure to meet metabolic demands.

If the ventricles beat chaotically, they can start to quiver and stop pumping blood entirely. This leads to blood pressure dropping to zero and potential immediate cardiac death.


Main types of arrhythmia:
	Tachycardia:		Resting heart rate above 100 bpm
	Bradycardia:		Resting heart rate below 60 bpm
	Premature Beats:	An early or skipped beat


What are potential causes of arrhythmia?
Arrhythmia can be triggered by factors like high blood pressure, coronary artery disease, electrolyte imbalances, stress, and drug use.
